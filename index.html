Steady aerobic rides only (Z2 to tempo, VI ≤ 1.06, 20 min+). Dots = rides, line = 30-day average. Rising = more watts per heartbeat: your aerobic engine is growing.

Aerobic decoupling (Pw:HR) trend--

Each point is a steady ride (Zone 2, tempo, sweet spot) with heart rate: how much power per heartbeat dropped from the first to the second half. 3.5% or less = base consolidated, under 5% = coupled; a falling trend at the same power means your aerobic base is improving.

Training terms explained

You do not need a sports science background. Start with the plain name; the abbreviation in brackets is what you see elsewhere in the app.

These are suggested names for your review. Training scores are estimates from recorded data: they cannot tell exactly how you feel, diagnose overtraining or guarantee recovery. More is not always better.

Your training and recovery
Fitness base (CTL)
Chronic Training LoadHow much training you have been used to over recent weeks. A rising score suggests you are doing more training; it does not prove you are faster.
Recent training strain (ATL)
Acute Training LoadHow much training you have done recently. A higher score suggests more strain to recover from; it is not a direct measure of tiredness.
Freshness (TSB)
Training Stress BalanceCompares your recent strain with your fitness base. It hints whether you might be rested or carrying tiredness. Check how you actually feel too.
Workout load (TSS)
Training Stress ScoreAn estimate of how demanding a ride was, combining how long and how hard you rode. Use it to compare your own rides, not as a score to beat.
Sustainable power (FTP)
Functional Threshold PowerAn estimate of the power you can hold during a long, hard effort. It helps the app set workout targets that suit you.
Fitness build rate (CTL ramp)
How quickly your fitness-base score is changing. A fast rise means you are adding training quickly, so check that you have enough recovery.
Understanding a ride
Overall effort power (NP)
Normalised PowerA calculated power value that gives extra weight to hard bursts. It helps describe how demanding an uneven ride was.
Relative ride effort (IF)
Intensity FactorCompares the ride's overall effort power with your sustainable power. It helps compare easy and hard rides for you.
Pacing variation (VI)
Variability IndexCompares overall effort power with average power. A higher value suggests a ride with more changes in effort.
Power for heart effort (EF)
Efficiency FactorCompares power with heart rate. It can help you track similar steady rides; heat, tiredness and missing sensor data can affect it.
Heart-rate drift (Pw:HR / decoupling)
Power-to-heart-rate relationshipChecks whether your heart needs to work harder for the same cycling effort later in a steady ride. Heat, drinks, food and fatigue can all play a part.
Best sustained power (MMP)
Mean Maximal PowerYour best average power over different effort lengths. This is the power curve: short efforts show bursts, longer efforts show staying power.
Personal best (PR)
Personal RecordYour best recorded result for a particular effort. It is a comparison with your own history.
Hard-effort capacity (VO2 max)
Maximum oxygen uptakeHow much oxygen your body can use during very hard exercise. Workouts with this name aim to improve your ability to ride hard; the workout itself does not measure this value.
Effort bands (Z1-Z7 / zones)
Groups ranging from very easy riding to short, maximum efforts. The app uses your sustainable power to set the bands. Easy rides should still feel easy.
Heart rate and recovery readings
Heart rate (HR / bpm)
Heart Rate / beats per minuteHow fast your heart is beating. Effort, heat, stress and tiredness can change it.
Resting heart rate (RHR)
Your heart rate while resting. Changes from your usual level can help you notice when you may need more recovery.
Heartbeat variation (HRV)
Heart Rate VariabilityThe small changes in time between heartbeats. Compare it with your own usual pattern; a single reading or a higher number is not automatically better.
Hard-effort heart rate (LTHR)
Lactate Threshold Heart RateAn estimate of your heart rate during a sustained hard effort. The app can use it to estimate the load of rides without power data.
Power, energy and pedalling
Cycling power (W)
WattsHow hard you are pushing the pedals right now. A workout target tells you the power to aim for.
Power compared with body weight (W/kg)
Watts per kilogramPower divided by body weight. It helps put climbing power in context; it does not describe all of your riding ability.
Pedalling speed (cadence / rpm)
Revolutions per minuteHow quickly you turn the pedals. Pedal smoothly rather than forcing a speed that feels uncomfortable.
Work done (kJ)
KilojoulesThe mechanical energy you put into the bike. Longer or harder rides usually add more.
Energy used (kcal)
KilocaloriesAn estimate of the energy your body used. This is different from the mechanical work you put into the bike.
Trainer and Bluetooth terms
Automatic resistance (ERG)
Ergometer modeThe trainer adjusts resistance to help you hold the workout's power target. Keep pedalling smoothly as the effort changes.
Match resistance to your power meter (PowerMatch)
Uses your separate power meter as the reference and gently adjusts trainer resistance to match the target. This helps keep your indoor and outdoor power readings consistent.
Trainer connection standard (FTMS)
Fitness Machine ServiceA Bluetooth language that compatible trainers use to share readings and accept control commands.
Power-meter connection standard (CPS)
Cycling Power ServiceA Bluetooth language used to share cycling power readings.
Bluetooth signal strength (RSSI)
Received Signal Strength IndicatorA clue to how strong the wireless signal is when the browser provides it. It is not a guarantee that the connection will stay stable.

FTP, weight and heart-rate limits drive every zone, target and W/kg in the app.

    Bluetooth troubleshooting
    ⚡ BLUETOOTH CONNECTION TROUBLESHOOTER
    • Wake Up Pedals: Spin cranks backwards 3–4 times. Assioma LEDs will flash (pedals sleep after 2m).
    • Disconnect Phone: Most sensors accept only one or two connections. Force-close the Favero Assioma app, Wahoo app, Polar Beat/Flow or Zwift on your phone/tablet.
    • Polar H10: Wet the electrodes and wear the strap (it only wakes on skin contact). If it was ever paired in Windows Settings > Bluetooth, remove it there, then connect from here. Failed first attempts are retried automatically.
    • Windows Bluetooth: Ensure Bluetooth is toggled ON in Windows Settings / Action Center.
    • Browser Popup: When you click Connect, look for the device popup at the top-left under the address bar and click Pair.
    • Use Launcher: For guaranteed permissions, double-click Launch-Apex-Velo.bat in your folder.

    RSSI appears only when the browser exposes advertisement data (Chrome experimental Web Bluetooth). A dropped link reconnects automatically with exponential backoff (1 s, 2 s, 4 s … capped at 30 s, 8 attempts).
